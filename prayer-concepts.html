Prayer Log — Three Concepts

Each concept approaches prayer differently. Review the mockups below and choose a direction.

Concept A

The Vigil Board

Prayer is a living conversation, not a closed document. This concept centers the lifecycle of a request — from first lifting it up, through days and weeks of faithful intercession, to the moment God answers. The board is always alive; you return to it, you mark things answered, you feel the weight of time.

Why this is different from the journal

  • State and lifecycle — a prayer is "open" until God answers it. No journal entry works like this.
  • The weight of time — "214 days in prayer" is a discipline statement. It shows faithfulness visually.
  • The answered moment — marking something answered is a small act of worship and testimony-keeping.
  • Sparse on words — prayer requests are brief and directed, not reflective paragraphs.
Concept B

The Daily Practice

Prayer is a discipline before it is a document. This concept mirrors the Bible intake feature — it cares primarily about whether you showed up. Log that you prayed today, bring your requests, track the streak. The emphasis is the habit of returning to God daily, not the prose of what you said.

Why this is different from the journal

  • Discipline-first — the heatmap and streak ask "did you show up?" not "what did you write?"
  • Checklist energy — praying through your list is an act, not a reflection. You check things off.
  • Persistent requests — requests carry forward each day until answered or removed, unlike journal entries.
  • Pairs naturally with the Bible intake page — same habit-tracking pattern, same data shape.
Concept C

The Intercession

Prayer is fundamentally about who you are bringing before God. This concept organizes your prayer life around people and concerns — Family, Friends, Church, World, Self. Instead of a flat list, your prayer life has shape and structure that reflects relationship.

"I urge, then, first of all, that petitions, prayers, intercession and thanksgiving be made for all people." — 1 Tim 2:1

Why this is different from the journal

  • Relational structure — your prayer life organized around people, not timestamps.
  • Intercession as the frame — the journal is about you and God; this is about bringing others to God.
  • Accordion groups — gives a sense of scope ("I'm praying for 9 people across 4 categories") without overwhelming.
  • The "214 days praying for Alex" detail makes the cost of faithful intercession visible and weighty.
